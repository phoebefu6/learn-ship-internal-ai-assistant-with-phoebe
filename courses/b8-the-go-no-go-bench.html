Every build step of the track, on one screen and computed in your browser. Tidewell Freight, a constructed 1,200-person freight forwarder, wants to ship a policy and ops helpdesk that answers staff from HR, IT and finance policy, cites the clause, and opens a ticket when it cannot answer. Part 1 · covers what the bench computes and where it can be fooled

One question, five steps, one board

5 min live

Each golden question travels the same path. The asker's group rides along with it, because who is asking decides what may be retrieved. Every step is code you can read in assist-live.js, and the Python reference reproduces all 269 numbers the ladder prints.

LiveBM25, exactly as the bench computes it3 min▶

For each distinct word in the question, a clause earns tf / (k1 x ((1 - b) + b x dl / avdl) + tf) x log((N - n + 0.5) / (n + 0.5)), and its score is the sum. tf is how often the word appears in the clause, dl the clause's length in words, avdl the average length (21.76 words here), N the number of clause versions in the store (49), n how many contain the word. It is equation 3.15 of Robertson and Zaragoza's 2009 review with the IDF of their equation 3.3. They suggest 1.2 < k1 < 2 and 0.5 < b < 0.8 as "reasonably good in many circumstances" and say the model gives no guidance on setting them; the bench uses k1 = 1.2 and b = 0.75.

Words are lower-cased, split on anything that is not a letter or digit, a short stopword list is dropped and a trailing "s" comes off words longer than three letters. There is no stemming and no synonym list, which is why "salary is paid" and "salaries are paid" can miss each other. That is a real property of lexical retrieval, and the reason session 2 links to dense and hybrid retrieval in the RAG course rather than pretending BM25 is enough.

LiveWhy the answers are pre-written, and why that does not weaken the bench2 min▶

A browser cannot run a language model, so each clause carries one constructed answer sentence and the assistant gives the answer of the clause it ranked first. That makes the bench an upper bound on generation quality: a real model can only add errors on top of the right clause. Everything the go/no-go reads, which clause was retrieved, whether the asker may read it, whether it is current, whether the assistant should have answered at all, is computed, and none of it depends on the wording of the answer.

Self-studyThe self-test, and the fault it is built to catch2 min▶

On load the bench checks five properties: the permission filter removes every leak, the version filter removes every stale citation, the full ladder ends in GO and the bare one in NO-GO, the break button produces a GO the register contradicts, and the thumbs grade passes the unsafe rung. Then it runs itself again with the permission filter switched off, a planted fault, and confirms the first property fails. A self-test that cannot fail measures nothing; the widget prints both results under the board.

Build-along · the bench

Climb the ladder, then try to fool it

★ 26 min · the main event

Click the rungs in order and say out loud which check fails before you read the board. The bench recomputes every number from the store on each click. Your numbers will match these, because nothing here is random: the store, the golden set and the thumbs signal are fixed constructed data.

Read the ticket log first. 600 constructed tickets in a quarter: 333 (55.5%) answered by pointing at a clause, 223 that needed a person to do something, 44 that nobody had written down. The assistant is for the first kind, opens tickets for the second, and must recognise the third. The golden set covers the topics of 100.0% of the policy-answerable tickets.

Rung 1, no permission filter. Top-3 hit rate 100.0%, MRR 0.913, and 18 leaks: eighteen questions pull a clause into the retrieved set that the asker may not read, such as the HR-only salary bands. Six answers cite a superseded version. Right hand-offs 0.0%, because nothing ever hands off. NO-GO, with six reasons.

Rung 2, add the permission filter. Leaks fall to 0. Stale citations rise from 6 to 7: when the managers-only improvement-plan clause is filtered out of a staff member's question (G46), the best remaining match is the superseded password clause. A filter changes what wins, not only what is removed. Still NO-GO.

Rung 3, add the current-version filter. Stale citations 0, MRR up to 0.988, correct answers 97.6%. And still NO-GO: medium-risk confident wrong 31.6%, low-risk 35.7%, right hand-offs 8.3%. The assistant answers the dog question, the birthday question and the staff party question with whatever clause scored highest.

Rung 4, add abstain and hand off. With the threshold at 3.0: GO. 0 leaks, 0 stale, confident wrong 0.0% · 0.0% · 7.1%, right hand-offs 91.7%, correct answers 90.5%. Pick G54 in the question box: the staff party question still scores 3.71 against the annual leave clause and gets a confident wrong answer, inside the low-risk limit. Pick G07: a real question about pay day, handed off at 2.77. Safe, and a miss.

Move the threshold. Drag "Abstain below" down to 0 and back up past 6. Low, and it behaves like rung 3. High, and coverage collapses until the board fails on correct answers. The threshold is a product decision with a measured price on both sides.

Press "Grade by thumbs-up instead" and go back to rung 1. 90.7% of questions got a thumbs-up, the bar is 75.0%: the thumbs grade prints GO on the assistant with 18 leaks and 6 stale citations. On rung 4 the rate drops to 81.5%, because staff thumb down hand-offs. The safer the assistant, the worse it grades. Switch the grade back.

Rung 4, press "Break it". The bench re-indexes version 4 of the expenses policy without its superseded flag. The board says GO, and it is false: the register shows 1 answer citing a superseded version while the pipeline sees 0. Pick G41 to see it: the SGD 50 per diem from version 4, not the SGD 60 daily allowance. Restore the flag before you leave.

Part 2 · covers what each rung fixes and what it leaves

The board, rung by rung

4 min live

Read the board as six independent checks. Each rung clears some and leaves others, and no single number on it tells you whether to ship. All values measured by the bench at the default threshold of 3.0.

LiveFour readings worth taking into the meeting4 min▶
  1. Retrieval quality was never the problem. Top-3 hit rate is 100.0% on every rung. A retrieval metric alone would have called rung 1 ready. The failures are about what the assistant was allowed to retrieve and what it did with a weak match.
  2. The permission filter belongs before ranking. On rung 1, 7 of the 18 leaking questions are answered from the clause the asker may not read. Filtering the answer afterwards would still have put that clause in front of the model.
  3. Staff ask in the old policy's words. Seven golden questions use superseded vocabulary: "holiday entitlement", "carry forward", "per diem", "remote work days". BM25 rewards the old wording, so without the version filter the old clause wins.
  4. The hand-off is what turns rung 3 into rung 4. Correct answers drop from 97.6% to 90.5%, and the board goes from NO-GO to GO. Answering less is the fix.
Self-studyWhat the risk tiers do, and where they come from2 min▶

Tidewell put each golden question in a tier by the cost of a confident wrong answer: pay, leave entitlements, conduct, security and expense limits are high (21 questions); hybrid work, purchasing, travel and the like are medium (19); how-to questions are low (14). The limits, 5%, 10% and 20%, are Tidewell's policy choice. They are not taken from any standard, and a bank or a hospital would set them lower. For how to derive a bar from the blast radius, the agent launch course on risk tiers goes further.

Part 3 · covers the two false GOs

A GO you should not believe

4 min live

A go/no-go is only as good as the signals its checks read. The bench builds two failures in which the board says GO and the assistant is not safe, and both have the same shape: the check reads a signal that the failure does not move.

LiveThe thumbs-up trap, and its honest limit3 min▶

The thumbs signal is constructed, and constructed to make a point: Tidewell's staff mostly thumb up answers and mostly thumb down hand-offs, whatever the answer says. Nobody can tell from a confident sentence that it came from a superseded clause or a document they were never meant to see. NIST's Generative AI Profile names this risk directly: confabulation does harm "when users believe false content - often due to the confident nature of the response". OWASP calls the user side of it overreliance (LLM09).

The honest limit: satisfaction is a real signal for tone, speed and whether the hand-off was graceful, and a team should collect it. It cannot be the go/no-go, because the failures that matter most, leaks and stale answers, look exactly like good answers to the person reading them.

LiveThe lost flag, and why the register must be the truth3 min▶

Somebody re-imports the old expenses PDF and the importer does not carry the superseded flag. Every check the pipeline runs reads the index, so every check passes, and the golden set grades the answer as right because it cites the right clause key, FIN-EXP-2. Only the version register, kept by the document owner, knows version 4 was retired on 2026-02-01. The bench keeps the two apart on purpose: the pipeline view and the register view agree on every rung until you press the button.

The fix is session 7's: the version check reads the owner's register, and a nightly job diffs the index against it. Lewis and colleagues' original RAG paper sells exactly this property, knowledge "can be easily updated at test time" by swapping the index. The same property means an index can be quietly wrong without retraining anything.

After the track

  2. For your own assistant, list which signal each go/no-go check reads. Circle any check that reads a signal written by the same pipeline it is checking.
  3. Move the bench's threshold until the board fails on coverage, and note the value. Then write one sentence for your leadership on what that trade is worth in tickets.
  4. Write the go/no-go policy for your assistant on one page: the checks, the limits by risk tier, who signs, and who may change a limit.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Builder session 8 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · On rung 3 the assistant answers 97.6% of answerable questions correctly with no leaks and no stale citations. Why is the board still NO-GO?

Rung 3 has no abstain step, so the dog, birthday and staff party questions get the best-scoring clause as an answer. Hit rate is 100.0% on every rung, and thumbs-up is 90.7% on rung 3, so A and D are wrong. Rung 4's hand-off is the fix.

2 · Graded by thumbs-up, rung 1 passes and rung 4 scores lower. What does that show?

Staff cannot see from a confident answer that it is stale or leaked, and they dislike hand-offs. Raising the bar (C) only reorders rungs on the same blind signal. D overstates it: satisfaction is a real signal for tone and hand-off quality, just not for the go/no-go.

3 · After "Break it", the pipeline verdict is GO and the register finds 1 stale citation. What should the version check read?

The index flag is exactly what broke, and the golden set grades by clause key, so both pass the stale answer. The register is the record the policy owner keeps; checking against it is what makes the false GO visible.
