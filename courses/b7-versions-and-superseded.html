Part 1 · covers the version register and the index flag

Register vs index

6 min live

There are two places that say whether version 4 of Tidewell's expenses policy is still in force. One is the register the financial controller keeps when a rule changes. The other is the superseded flag the index copied when the clause was loaded. Every filter and every check in the pipeline reads the second, because it is fast. Only the first is the truth.

LiveTwo records, two jobs3 min▶

The register is a short list the document owner keeps: for every clause version, whether it is current and, if not, the day it was retired. It changes when a rule changes, and only the owner changes it. The index is what retrieval reads: clause text, BM25 statistics and a copy of the superseded flag, so the current-version filter can drop old versions before ranking without a second lookup.

The copy is a cache, and a cache is right only until someone reloads it carelessly. Treat the register as the source of truth and the index flag as a fast, checkable copy of it. That one sentence decides every design choice below: which record the stale-citation check reads, who is paged when the two disagree, and what the go/no-go believes.

Four fields, one row per version. It is deliberately smaller than the clause store: the register says nothing about text or access, only about time.

Self-studyWhy not keep only the index?2 min▶

Because the index is rebuilt by machines and the register is written by the person who changed the policy. Importers drop fields, default booleans to false, and re-read old PDFs from shared folders. A single record that both the importer and the owner write has no way to say which of them was wrong. Two records that must agree turn a silent fault into a visible mismatch, which is the whole point of Part 3.

Real world

The property that makes RAG easy to update also makes it easy to break. Lewis and colleagues' 2020 paper that named retrieval-augmented generation showed that the model's knowledge "can be easily updated at test time" by replacing the retrieved index, with no retraining. That is why Tidewell can change its expenses rule overnight. It is also why one careless re-import can change the rule back overnight, with no model change for anyone to review. The register is the record that survives the swap.

Part 2 · covers stale citations and the old vocabulary

Old words, old answers

6 min live

Staff ask in the words of the rule they learned. Tidewell renamed "per diem" to "daily allowance", but a question about the per diem still contains "per diem", and so does version 4 of the clause. A keyword retriever does what it is built to do and ranks the old clause first. Without a version filter, the answer is last year's number, with a real citation attached.

LiveSix stale citations on rung 13 min▶

On the bench's first rung, no filters at all, six golden questions are answered from a superseded version: G14, G36, G37, G38, G39 and G41. They cite the old password, holiday, carry forward, remote work and per diem versions. Every one of those citations is real: the clause exists, it is in the retrieved set, and it says what the answer says. It was simply retired.

Look at the words. G36 asks about "holiday entitlement", G37 about carrying "forward", G38 about "remote work days", G41 about the "per diem". Tidewell's current clauses say annual leave, carrying over, working from home and daily allowance. BM25 rewards the words that appear in both question and clause, and the old clause is the one that shares them.

LiveThe seventh, after the permission filter2 min▶

Rung 2 adds the permission filter from session 5. Leaks fall from 18 to 0, and stale citations rise from 6 to 7. The new one is G46: a staff member asks how long a performance improvement plan lasts. The clause that answers it is for managers only, so the filter removes it, and the best remaining match is the superseded IT-ACC-1 v3 password clause. A filter changes what wins, not only what is removed. That is why every filter is measured on the whole golden set, never on the questions it was written for.

Self-studyWhy a key-only grade misses it2 min▶

Version 4 and version 5 of the meals rule share the key FIN-EXP-2. A grader that asks "did the answer cite the right clause?" by key alone marks the SGD 50 answer right. Stale is a property of the version, so the stale-citation check must compare the cited clause id, key and version together, against a record of which versions are retired. On rung 3, with the current-version filter on, stale citations drop to 0, MRR rises from 0.913 to 0.988 and correct answers reach 41 of 42 (97.6%).

Real world

A stale answer reads exactly like a right one. NIST AI 600-1 defines confabulation as "The production of confidently stated but erroneous or false content", and notes that the risk arises when users believe false content, often because of the confident nature of the response. A stale citation is a cousin of that: the content was true once and is cited properly, so nothing on the screen signals a problem. In the constructed Tidewell case, a staff member asking G41 claims SGD 50 a day for meals and never learns the allowance is now SGD 60.

Part 3 · covers the break button and the scheduled register vs index check

The nightly diff

5 min live

The current-version filter fixes stale citations only while the index flag is right. Somebody re-imports the old expenses PDF, the importer does not carry the superseded flag, and version 4 is current again in the index. The bench's break button does exactly that, and the go/no-go board turns green on an assistant that is wrong. Only a check that reads the register can see it.

LivePress the break button3 min▶

Open the bench in session 8, go to rung 4 and press "Break it". The bench re-indexes version 4 of the expenses policy without its superseded flag. The pipeline verdict is GO. The register verdict is NO-GO: 1 answer cites a superseded version, G41, the SGD 50 per diem from v4 instead of the SGD 60 daily allowance in v5. The pipeline sees 0 stale citations. Correct answers fall to 37 of 42 (88.1%) and MRR to 0.964, but both still clear the bar.

Step down to rung 3 with the break still on: the register finds 2 stale citations, G38 and G41, both citing FIN-EXP-2 v4, and the pipeline again sees 0. At rung 4, G38 is one of the questions handed off, which is why only G41 shows there. Restore the flag before you leave.

LiveFour things the diff compares2 min▶
  • Flag differs. The register says superseded, the index says current, or the reverse. The break button's fault.
  • Registered, not indexed. A new current version the owner recorded but nobody loaded. Staff get the previous rule or a hand-off.
  • Indexed, not registered. A draft or a stray file that answers questions and has no owner.
  • An answer cited a retired version. Yesterday's answer log, checked against the register, dates any stale answer that already went out.

Run it every night and on every load. On any problem the job fails, the owner is told, and the safe default holds: the version filter excludes whatever the register says is retired, whatever the index flag says.

Self-studyWhere this stops2 min▶

This session takes one side: one register, one scheduled diff and one stale-citation check, in plain Python. The after-launch process around it, who reviews what each week and when a change reopens the go/no-go, belongs to the agent launch course's after-launch session. Tracing, dashboards and alerting in production belong to the AI observability course.

Real world

Decide in advance whether a stale answer is an incident. Singapore's Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI (IMDA and AI Verify Foundation, 2024) lists incident reporting among its nine dimensions and says that defining "severe AI incidents" or setting the materiality threshold for formal reporting "is therefore key". G41 is a high-risk question on Tidewell's constructed golden set. Whether one stale per diem answer is a fix-tomorrow or a report-today is Tidewell's call, and it should be written down before the diff first fails, not after.

Build-along

A register vs index diff that fails the night

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Plain Python, standard library only. A constructed register of seven clause versions, an index after a careless re-import with four faults planted on purpose, and a three-line answer log. The script must find every fault and exit with a failure so a scheduler can act on it. Run it from an empty scratch folder.

Write the register the owner keeps. One row per clause version: the id (key plus version), whether it is superseded, and the day it was retired. Seven constructed rows from Tidewell's expenses, leave and hybrid work policies.

★ nightly_diff.py · part 1, the register (constructed)# The owner's version register: the truth (constructed) register = [ {"id": "FIN-EXP-1@v5", "superseded": False, "retired": None}, {"id": "FIN-EXP-1@v4", "superseded": True, "retired": "2026-02-01"}, {"id": "FIN-EXP-2@v5", "superseded": False, "retired": None}, {"id": "FIN-EXP-2@v4", "superseded": True, "retired": "2026-02-01"}, {"id": "HR-LV-1@v3", "superseded": False, "retired": None}, {"id": "HR-LV-1@v2", "superseded": True, "retired": "2026-01-01"}, {"id": "HR-WFH-1@v2", "superseded": False, "retired": None}, ]

Write the index as last night's re-import left it. Four faults planted: both expenses v4 flags lost, the current hybrid work clause never loaded, and a password draft that nobody registered.

★ nightly_diff.py · part 2, the index (constructed)# What the index holds after last night's re-import (constructed) index = { "FIN-EXP-1@v5": False, "FIN-EXP-1@v4": False, # flag lost on re-import "FIN-EXP-2@v5": False, "FIN-EXP-2@v4": False, # flag lost on re-import "HR-LV-1@v3": False, "HR-LV-1@v2": True, "IT-ACC-1@v5": False, # a draft nobody registered }

Checks one to three: compare the two records both ways. Walk the register and look each version up in the index, then walk the index and look for anything the register has never heard of.

★ nightly_diff.py · part 3, register vs indexproblems = [] truth = {r["id"]: r for r in register} for cid, r in truth.items(): if cid not in index: problems.append(f"{cid}: in register, missing from index") elif index[cid] != r["superseded"]: problems.append(f"{cid}: register superseded={r['superseded']}, index says {index[cid]}") for cid in index: if cid not in truth: problems.append(f"{cid}: in index, unknown to register")

Check four: did any answer already cite a retired version? Read the day's answer log, look each cited id up in the register, not the index, and exit non-zero on any problem so the scheduler marks the night failed.

★ nightly_diff.py · part 4, the answer log and the exit code# Today's answers and the clause each one cited (constructed log) answers = [("G36", "HR-LV-1@v3"), ("G41", "FIN-EXP-2@v4"), ("G40", "FIN-EXP-1@v5")] for qid, cid in answers: r = truth.get(cid) if r and r["superseded"]: problems.append(f"{qid}: cited {cid}, retired {r['retired']}") print(len(register), "registered,", len(index), "indexed,", len(problems), "problems") for p in problems: print(" " + p) raise SystemExit(1 if problems else 0)

Run it and read the five lines. python3 nightly_diff.py; echo "exit $?" printed exactly this. Fewer than five problems means a check is broken; more means a fault you did not plant. On your own register and index, your numbers will differ.

★ Output7 registered, 7 indexed, 5 problems FIN-EXP-1@v4: register superseded=True, index says False FIN-EXP-2@v4: register superseded=True, index says False HR-WFH-1@v2: in register, missing from index IT-ACC-1@v5: in index, unknown to register G41: cited FIN-EXP-2@v4, retired 2026-02-01 exit 1

Fix the index, never the register. Restore both v4 flags from the register, load HR-WFH-1@v2, and remove IT-ACC-1@v5 until its owner registers it. Re-run: the four index problems clear. The G41 line stays, because that answer already went out; it goes to the expenses owner to decide whether to correct it. Then schedule the script nightly and as the last step of every load.

Three questions

Clear all three and Builder session 7 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Which record should decide whether a clause version may answer?

The index flag is fast, and it is exactly what a careless re-import breaks. The register is written by the person who changed the rule, so it is the truth the copy is checked against. D is how the SGD 50 per diem got cited in the first place.

2 · On the bench, stale citations go from 6 on rung 1 to 7 on rung 2. Why did adding the permission filter add one?

A filter changes what wins, not only what is removed. The store still holds 7 superseded versions on every rung; what changed is which clause is left at the top for one staff question.

3 · After "Break it" at rung 4, the pipeline says GO with 0 stale citations. What catches the G41 answer?

v4 and v5 share the key FIN-EXP-2, so a key-only grade passes the SGD 50 answer, and staff cannot see that a confident answer is stale. Only the register knows v4 was retired on 2026-02-01.
