Part 1 · covers the three kinds of helpdesk ticket

Three bins, three behaviours

6 min live

Sort tickets by what it took to close them, not by department. A ticket closed by pasting a policy link is a different job from one closed by resetting a token, and both differ from one closed by a manager making a rule up on the spot.

BinThe testTidewell examplesWhat the assistant does
Policy lookupThe agent who closed it pasted or paraphrased a written clauseSick leave days, hotel cap, MFA on the VPNAnswers from the clause and cites it

Tidewell's constructed quarter: 333 policy lookups (55.5%), 223 that needed a person, 44 with no written answer. Sort a sample of your own before you trust any split, including this one: the bins depend on how well your policies are written, which is exactly what you are trying to find out.

LiveWhy the second bin is not the assistant's job yet3 min▶

An assistant that resets passwords or edits payroll is an agent with write access to systems, which is a different risk class with its own course. Tidewell's assistant takes exactly one action: it opens a ticket. That keeps the second bin on the people who own those systems, and the assistant's contribution there is a well-formed ticket: the question, who asked, and the clauses it looked at. Session 6 of the builder track builds that one tool; the AI agents course owns everything beyond it.

Real world

The deflection number nobody can source. Internal helpdesk assistants are usually sold on a "deflection rate", the share of questions that never became a ticket. No credible public benchmark for it was found for these pages; the figures in circulation are vendor-reported. Deflection also counts a person who gave up as a success. Measure the first bin's resolution instead, and whether the same person opened a ticket on the same topic soon after.

Part 2 · covers why the no-written-answer bin is the dangerous one

A ranking always has a winner

6 min live

An assistant that answers from documents first ranks the documents against the question. A ranking of 49 clauses always has a first place, even when no clause is about the question. Unless someone builds a rule for "the best match is not good enough", the assistant answers from it.

LiveWhy a fluent wrong answer is worse than no answer3 min▶

NIST's Generative AI Profile calls this risk confabulation: "the production of confidently stated but erroneous or false content", and it locates the harm where users believe it, "often due to the confident nature of the response". An internal assistant makes this worse in one specific way: it cites a real clause. The staff party answer would cite Tidewell's annual leave policy, correctly quoted and irrelevant. A citation that exists is not a citation that answers.

The fix is a leader decision before it is a technical one: the assistant is allowed, and expected, to say "I cannot answer that from policy" and open a ticket. Session 4 is about that sentence. On the bench the hand-off line sits at a top score of 3.0; G54 still clears it with 3.71 and gets a confident wrong answer, which is why the line is a trade, not a cure.

Self-studyThe third bin is also a to-do list for policy owners2 min▶

Forty-four tickets with no written answer is 44 times a person made a rule up, probably not the same rule each time. Some of those topics deserve a policy (meeting rooms, guests, a birthday day off if staff keep asking). Others deserve a sentence on the intranet saying there is no policy. Either way, the list of no-written-answer topics is a by-product of scoping an assistant that is worth more than the assistant to some organisations.

Part 3 · covers where the helpdesk minutes go and what an answering assistant can reach

Count minutes, not tickets

5 min live

The first bin has the most tickets. It does not have the most minutes. Before anyone promises a saving, put the minutes next to the counts and draw a line round what an assistant that only answers can reach.

LiveWhat to put on the business case, and what to leave off3 min▶

On Tidewell's constructed quarter, policy lookups took 3,707 of 13,437 helpdesk minutes. That is the ceiling for an assistant that answers questions, before you subtract the questions it should hand off and the ones it gets wrong. The 9,064 minutes of action tickets are untouched by answering, whatever the demo suggested.

The published evidence on AI help for support work does not settle the size of the prize either. In one field study of 5,179 customer support agents, Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond (NBER Working Paper 31161) found issues resolved per hour rose 14% on average, 34% for novice and low-skilled workers, with minimal impact on the most experienced. A study of 25,000 Danish workers in exposed occupations by Humlum and Vestergaard (BFI Working Paper 2025-56) found average time savings of 3% and no measurable effect on earnings or recorded hours. Different settings, outcomes and horizons; session 6 takes the disagreement apart. Neither tells you Tidewell's number. Your ticket log does.

Self-studyTopics, and why the top five are not the scope3 min▶

Tidewell's biggest topics in the quarter were access (98 tickets), leave (95), expenses (75), devices (74), software (65) and pay (51). Access and devices are mostly action tickets: resets and broken hardware. Leave and expenses are mostly lookups. A scope written as "the top five topics" would promise the assistant a job it cannot do on two of them. Scope by bin first, then by topic inside the first bin.

Build-along

Your scope sentence, from fifty tickets

★ 20 min · everyone builds

You need an export of recent tickets with the subject, the team and how each was closed; fifty is enough to see the shape. No data to hand? Use Tidewell's numbers on this page and write the sentence for Tidewell. Your numbers will differ, which is the point.

Pull fifty recent tickets across HR, IT and finance. Take them from one ordinary month, not a launch or a payroll incident. Keep subject, team, how it was closed and minutes spent if your tool records them.

Tag every ticket P, A or N. P if a written clause closed it. A if someone had to do something in a system or make a judgement. N if the answer exists nowhere in writing. Disagree with a colleague on five of them and settle the rule; the disagreements are where your policies are vague.

★ Block 1 · the tags, Tidewell styleP "How many days sick leave before I need a certificate?" HR 8 min A "Laptop will not boot after update" IT 45 min P "Hotel limit for the Jakarta trip?" Fin 7 min N "Can I bring my dog on Fridays?" HR 12 min

Tally counts and minutes per tag. Then put the P minutes over the total. That fraction is the most an answering assistant can take over, before any hand-offs.

Rank the P topics and name the source document and owner for each. A topic whose policy has no named owner goes on a waiting list: an assistant that cites a document nobody maintains will be quoting it long after it stops being true, which is session 6's problem.

Write the sentence. Read it to the heads of HR, IT and finance. If any of them says "it should also do X", ask which bin X lives in.

★ The scope sentenceThe assistant answers staff questions on [leave, hybrid work, expenses, travel] from [the leave, hybrid work, expenses and travel policies], citing the clause. It opens a ticket for [resets, repairs and payroll corrections], and says it cannot answer from policy, and opens a ticket, for everything else.
✗ The version that sounds like a strategy and scopes nothing An AI-powered employee experience assistant that answers any question about HR, IT and finance, available 24/7, reducing helpdesk tickets.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · A ticket reads "laptop will not boot after the update" and was closed by an IT engineer reimaging the machine. Which bin is it, and what does the assistant do?

Someone had to act on a system, so it is an action ticket. The assistant's one tool is opening a ticket, and its value here is a well-formed one. A is the trap: a devices clause exists, but no clause boots a laptop.

2 · Why is the no-written-answer bin the most dangerous, though it is the smallest?

C is the intuition and it is wrong: retrieval finds something for almost every question. The dog question's best match is the disciplinary hearing clause. NIST calls the result confabulation, and the harm comes from the confidence.

3 · Policy lookups are 333 of Tidewell's 600 tickets but 3,707 of 13,437 minutes. What should the business case claim?

Ticket share is not time share: action tickets are fewer and longer. Opening a ticket does not close it. And no credible public deflection benchmark was found; the figures in circulation are vendor-reported.
