Ship It: Internal AI Assistant · Leader session 3 of 6

Who may see what

A policy assistant reads every clause it is given, for whoever is asking. Tidewell Freight, a constructed 1,200-person freight forwarder, keeps salary bands, spending limits and improvement-plan procedure in the same store as the leave policy. Run its golden questions with no permission filter and 18 of them pull a clause into the answer that the asker may not read. Tonight is the leader's half of that problem: count the leaks, decide where the filter sits, and decide who sets access. You leave with an access matrix your HR, IT and finance owners have signed.

Part 1 · covers what a leak is and how many a plain retriever produces

Leaks, counted

6 min live

A retriever ranks clauses by how well their words match the question. It has no idea who is asking. So a staff member's question that happens to share words with a managers-only clause gets that clause in its top three, and the answer is written from what was retrieved.

LiveWhat counts as a leak, so two people count the same way4 min▶
CountThe testTidewell, rung 1
LeakA clause the asker's group may not read is in the retrieved top 318 of 54 golden questions
Leaked answerThe answer itself is written from that clause7 of those 18
Restricted questionA clause answers it, but the asker may not read the clause6 of 54 by design

Read the first column carefully. Only 6 golden questions were written to probe restricted clauses, yet 18 leak. The rest are ordinary questions whose wording happens to overlap a restricted clause: a word like "plan", "approve" or "limit" is enough. You cannot list the dangerous questions in advance, which is why the count has to come from running the whole golden set, every time anything changes.

Count the leak, not just the leaked answer. The other 11 questions were answered from a clause the asker could read, but the forbidden one was still handed to the generator. Whether it surfaces depends on the phrasing and the next model update, and you will not be told when that changes.

Self-studyWhy the quality numbers cannot see a leak3 min▶

Compare rung 1 (no permission filter) with rung 2 (permission filter on) on the bench. Correct answers: 35 of 42 on both. Top-3 hit rate: 100.0% on both. Thumbs-up: 90.7% on both. Leaks: 18, then 0. The filter moves none of the numbers a demo or a satisfaction survey reports, and it is the difference between an assistant you can launch and one you cannot.

That is also why the person who receives a leaked answer will not report it. A staff member told how long an improvement plan lasts got a fluent, cited, correct-looking answer. Nothing in the reply says "you were not supposed to see this". Leaks are found by counting against an access matrix, never by waiting for complaints.

Real world

OWASP lists it second. The OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 puts Sensitive Information Disclosure at LLM02. Among its prevention steps: limit access to sensitive data "based on the principle of least privilege". For a policy assistant, least privilege has a concrete meaning: the assistant may only retrieve, on a person's behalf, what that person could already open themselves.

Part 2 · covers where the permission filter must sit and what it changes

Filter before ranking

6 min live

There are two places a team can try to stop a leak. One is inside retrieval: remove every clause the asker may not read before anything is ranked. The other is at the end: let the assistant answer, then scan the answer and redact. Only the first one works, and the reason is mechanical, not a matter of taste.

LiveWhy redacting the answer fails3 min▶

Redaction works on the finished text. To catch a leak there, the scanner has to recognise restricted content after it has been paraphrased, summarised or blended with a readable clause. "Grade 7 sits between two numbers" can be reworded in many ways; a pattern that catches one misses the next. And in 11 of Tidewell's 18 leaking questions the forbidden clause shaped nothing visible in the answer at all, so there is nothing to redact and nothing to log.

The filter inside retrieval asks a much simpler question, once per clause: may this person read this? The answer is a lookup against the asker's group and the clause's access field, not a judgement about prose. That is why it can be tested to exactly zero. On the bench, adding it takes leaks from 18 to 0 and leaked answers from 7 to 0, with no change to the 35 correct answers.

LiveA filter changes what wins3 min▶

Turning the filter on is not free of side effects. Take G46 again, the staff member asking how long an improvement plan lasts. With the managers-only clause gone, the best remaining match is the superseded IT password clause, an old version still sitting in the store. Stale citations rise from 6 at rung 1 to 7 at rung 2.

The leader lesson: every filter you add reorders the survivors, so every filter change needs the whole golden set rerun, not a spot check of the question that prompted it. The next rung on the bench adds a current-version filter and the stale count falls to 0. Session 6 returns to keeping versions true after launch.

Self-studyWhich side of the line this page takes2 min▶

This page takes the decision and the count: where the filter sits, and that the golden set must show zero leaks. Access models themselves (roles, attributes, row-level rules) belong to the data access control course, with the mechanism in its row-level security session. A poisoned policy document is a different attack, indirect prompt injection; OWASP notes under LLM01 that RAG and fine-tuning "do not fully mitigate prompt injection vulnerabilities", and the AI red team course owns it. Real world

OWASP's wording is a specification. Under LLM08, Vector and Embedding Weaknesses, the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 lists as a prevention step: "Implement fine-grained access controls and permission-aware vector and embedding stores." Permission-aware means the store knows who is asking when it returns results. A store that returns everything and trusts a later step to tidy up is exactly what that line warns against.

Part 3 · covers who decides each clause's access group, and why per clause

Who sets access

5 min live

A filter is only as right as the access field it reads. The usual shortcut is to inherit access from wherever the document is stored. Policies are not written in folder-sized pieces: one document can hold a clause every employee needs and a clause only HR may read.

LiveOwner-set per clause, never folder-inherited3 min▶

Tidewell's constructed store gives every clause a stable key, a version, an effective date, a superseded flag, an owner and an access group. The owner is a named role: the reward manager for pay, the IT security manager for privileged access, the chief financial officer for spending limits. That owner, not the IT team and not the build team, says which group may read each clause, because the owner is the only person who knows why the clause is restricted.

Folder inheritance fails in both directions. Make the pay folder HR only and staff can no longer ask about pay day or overtime; make it all staff and the salary bands go with it. Either way, nobody decided; the storage layout decided. A shared-drive permission was set for people browsing files, not for an assistant that quotes one paragraph out of a document to whoever asks.

Keep the number of groups small. Every new group is a new column in the matrix you build tonight and a new set of test questions. If an owner asks for a group of one person, the clause probably does not belong in a staff assistant at all.

Self-studyWhere the law comes in, and where it does not2 min▶

Tidewell's policies are constructed and are not legal advice. Whether a clause must be restricted because it holds personal data is a question for your data protection lead and the law that applies to you; the PDPA course and the GDPR course own that. Most restrictions in a policy store are not legal at all: they are business confidentiality, like spending limits and supplier terms. The owner decides those, and the matrix records the decision.

Real world

A constructed Tidewell case: the reorganised drive. In this constructed story, IT tidies the shared drive and moves the pay and grading policy into an HR-only folder. An assistant that inherited folder access starts answering "I cannot find a policy on that" to staff asking about pay day, and the helpdesk tickets come back. Nobody changed a policy; somebody moved a file. Per-clause access, signed by the reward manager, would not have moved.

Build-along

An access matrix for five askers and nine restricted clauses

★ 20 min · everyone builds

You need the list of policy documents in your scope sentence from session 1 and a name for each owner. No data to hand? Build Tidewell's matrix from the tables on this page; it is constructed, and your numbers will differ. The matrix is the artifact the build team tests against.

List every clause that is not for all staff. Work from the documents named in your scope sentence. For each restricted clause write its key, a short title and its owner by role. A clause with no owner cannot be restricted correctly, so it goes on the waiting list with session 2's unowned topics.

★ Block 1 · restricted clauses, Tidewell stylekey title owner HR-PAY-2 Salary bands Reward manager HR-PERF-1 Performance improvement plan HR business partner lead FIN-DOA-1 Spending limits Chief financial officer IT-ADM-1 Local admin rights IT security manager

Pick the groups, and keep them few. Start from the groups your HR system already knows: all staff, managers, and one per function that holds confidential procedure. Tidewell uses five. Write who each group covers in one line, including whether HR sits inside "managers".

Have each owner fill their own rows. Send each owner only their clauses and the group list. The owner picks one group per clause and signs the row. Where an owner hesitates, ask the question that settles it: would you hand this paragraph to a new joiner who asked?

Write one test question for every "no" that matters. Write it in the words of someone who must not get the answer, the way G46 asks "how long does a performance improvement plan last?" as a staff member. These join the golden set as restricted questions. Add a few ordinary questions that share words with a restricted clause, because that is where most of Tidewell's 18 leaks came from.

★ Block 3 · restricted test questionsasker question must not cite staff How long does a performance improvement plan last? HR-PERF-1 staff What spending limit does a director have? FIN-DOA-1 staff How do I get local admin rights on my laptop? IT-ADM-1

Write the line for the build team. One sentence, no options. The zero is Tidewell's policy choice, not an industry standard; set yours deliberately and write down who chose it.

★ The decision lineThe assistant filters clauses by the asker's group inside retrieval, before ranking. Access is set per clause by the clause owner, recorded in the access matrix. The golden set, including one restricted question per "no" cell, must show 0 leaks before launch and after every change to the store, the filter or the matrix.

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 3 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · On Tidewell's bench with no permission filter, 18 of 54 golden questions leak, but only 7 are answered from the forbidden clause. Why count all 18?

A leak is a retrieval event, not an answer event. D is the trap: only 6 golden questions were written as restricted, so most leaks came from ordinary questions that share words with a restricted clause.

2 · A supplier proposes to scan every answer and redact salary figures before it reaches the user. What is the problem?

The filter inside retrieval asks one checkable question per clause: may this person read it? OWASP LLM08 asks for permission-aware stores for that reason. C assumes you can list every way a figure can be reworded.

3 · Tidewell's pay and grading policy holds pay day, overtime, salary review and salary bands. Who should set each clause's access group?

Folder inheritance either hides pay day from staff or shows salary bands to everyone. Only the owner knows why a clause is restricted, so the owner signs the row.
