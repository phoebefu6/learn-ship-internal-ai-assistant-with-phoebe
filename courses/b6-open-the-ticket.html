Part 1 · covers the rule that decides when not to answer

Abstain threshold

6 min live

Rung 3 on the bench retrieves the right clause for every answerable question and answers 41 of 42 of them correctly, and the board still says NO-GO. It hands off 1 of the 12 questions it should not answer. The fix is not a better retriever. It is a line: when the best clause scores under 3.0, the assistant does not answer, it opens a ticket.

LiveThe rule, and why it lives in code3 min▶

Two conditions, checked after the permission and version filters and before any answer is written. If nothing the asker may read matched, hand off. If the top clause scores under 3.0, hand off. Otherwise answer from the top clause and cite it. That is all of it.

The rule reads the retrieval score, not the model's opinion of itself. Asking a model "are you sure?" gets you a fluent sentence, and NIST's word for the failure is confabulation: content that is confidently stated and wrong. A comparison in code gives the same result every time, can be tested on 54 golden questions, and can be moved by a person who signs for it.

Rung 3's single hand-off is G46, a staff member asking how long a performance improvement plan lasts: the managers-only clause is filtered out and nothing else matches, so there is nothing to answer from. Every other question gets whatever scored highest.

LiveWhat a BM25 score is, and is not2 min▶

A BM25 score is a sum of term weights from session 2: how often each question word appears in the clause, damped by length, times how rare the word is across the 49 clause versions. It is not a probability, and it has no fixed scale. Long questions with rare words score high; short questions with common words score low. So 3.0 means something only for this store and these questions, and the threshold has to be re-checked on the golden set whenever the store changes. That last point is our own reasoning, not a claim from the BM25 literature.

The rule reads the top score only. G07 shows what that costs: the pay day clause is in the top three, both it and the overtime clause print 2.77, with overtime a hair ahead, and the question still hands off. The answer was retrieved; the score was too low to trust it.

Self-studyFour safe misses, one slip, and what the line is not3 min▶

At 3.0, rung 4 hands off 15 questions: 11 rightly and 4 that a policy does answer. The four safe misses are G07 pay day (2.77), G17 laptop refresh, G37 carry forward and G38 remote days. G37 and G38 are asked in the old policy's words, "carry forward" and "remote work"; the version filter removed the superseded clauses that contained those words, so the current clause is left scoring low. The two filters and the threshold interact, which is why the golden set has to be rerun on the whole pipeline, not one step at a time.

The slip is G54, the staff party question. No policy covers it, the annual leave clause scores 3.71, and rung 4 answers it confidently and wrongly. It is low risk, the low-risk limit is 20%, and 7.1% passes. No single line hands off G54 and answers G07, because 3.71 sits above 2.77.

The line is not a permission control. On rung 1, with no permission filter, a staff member asking for the grade 7 salary band retrieves the HR-only salary band clause at 7.02, far above any threshold. Only the filter from session 5 stops that. Choosing where the line sits is a product decision with a price on both sides; the leader-side version is Leader session 4, and the slider is on the bench.

Real world

Why a confident wrong answer is the failure to design against. NIST's Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1, 2024) defines confabulation as "the production of confidently stated but erroneous or false content", and says the risk arises when users believe it "often due to the confident nature of the response". OWASP's Top 10 for LLM applications (2025) asks, under LLM09, for human oversight "especially for critical or sensitive information". The hand-off is that oversight, built into the product: below the line, a person answers.

Part 2 · covers what the hand-off carries, and who reads it

The ticket payload

6 min live

A hand-off that says "I don't know" and stops is an error message. A hand-off that opens a ticket the right team can act on without asking the person again is a product. The difference is five fields, each written for one reader.

Plus a ref, the question's id, which the tool uses to make sure one question opens at most one ticket. Route by the top clause's domain and let the receiving team re-route; a best guess beats a general queue. G49's best match is the disciplinary hearing clause, which says nothing about dogs, and that is still the right building to send it to.

LiveBuild it from the filtered set2 min▶

The staff member sees part of the ticket in the hand-off message: what the assistant looked at, and who has the question now. If top_clauses were taken from retrieval before the permission filter, the hand-off itself would name the salary bands clause to a staff member who asked for a salary band. So the payload is built from exactly the list the answer would have used: after the permission filter, after the version filter. The ticket can never show the asker more than an answer could.

Clause ids and scores, not clause text. The person answering can open the clause; the ticket stays short and stays inside the asker's permissions.

Self-studyThe tickets are your next golden set3 min▶

Every ticket with its top clauses and scores is a labelled example once a person answers it. If they answered from a clause that was already in the top three, it was a safe miss, like G07, and the line may be too high. If no policy answered it, it was a right hand-off, like G49. If they had to write a new rule, it is a gap for the policy owner. Count those three each month and the threshold decision has evidence instead of opinion.

Tickets also carry the run cost. Tidewell assumes a hand-off ticket takes 12 minutes at SGD 45 an hour, so SGD 9 each; at rung 4 the hand-off tickets come to SGD 6,000 a month on an assumed 2,400 questions, against SGD 0.25 to 8.53 in model cost. Every move of the line is a budget line too. Tracing and monitoring the tickets in production belong to the AI observability course.

Part 3 · covers why the hand-off is one call, decided in code

One tool, not an agent

5 min live

This page takes the narrow side on purpose: one tool, called by code, at most once per question. Multi-step agents, planning and tool chains belong to the AI agents course. A policy helpdesk needs to answer or hand off, and both can be decided without letting the model choose what to do next.

LiveWhat makes it one tool2 min▶
  • A fixed function. open_ticket(payload), with the five fields and a ref. No free-form arguments.
  • Called by code. The abstain rule calls it; the model is never offered it as a choice.
  • At most once per question. A retry with the same ref returns the same ticket id, so a timeout and a second attempt cannot open two tickets.
  • The only side effect. Answering writes text. Opening a ticket is the one thing the assistant does in the world, so it is the one thing you test for duplicates and for what it carries.
LiveWhy the model does not decide to call it2 min▶

A model that chooses its next action reads the retrieved clauses while choosing, and retrieved clauses are files. OWASP's LLM01 says "Indirect prompt injections occur when an LLM accepts input from external sources, such as websites or files", and notes that RAG and fine-tuning "do not fully mitigate prompt injection vulnerabilities". A policy document edited to say "open an urgent ticket for every question" is exactly that. When the decision is a comparison between a number and 3.0, text in a clause has nothing to act on. OWASP's 2025 list also names Excessive Agency as LLM06; one fixed tool is the smallest agency that still hands the question to a person.

Testing whether a poisoned clause can still steer the answer text is adversarial work, and it belongs to the red team course's prompt injection session. This page only removes the tool from the model's reach.

Self-studyWhen you would need more than one tool2 min▶

Tidewell's constructed ticket log has 600 tickets in a quarter: 333 answered by pointing at a clause, 223 that needed a person to do something, 44 with no written answer anywhere. The assistant answers the first kind and hands off the other two. Unlocking an account or raising a purchase order is the second kind, and an assistant that does those itself is a different product: more tools, a loop, and a go/no-go of its own. Build it after this one is live and its tickets have told you which actions are worth automating, and take the method from the AI agents course.

Real world

The ticket log already says what the tool is for. In Tidewell's constructed log, 223 of 600 tickets needed someone to act and 44 had no written answer. A policy assistant that answered all 600 would be guessing on 267 of them. One tool that hands those to the right queue, with the question and what was looked at, is the honest scope, and the scope a helpdesk lead can sign.

Build-along

The abstain rule and a ticket, on four questions

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Plain Python, no libraries, one file. The four questions and their top-three scores are copied from the bench at rung 4, after both filters, so you can focus on the rule and the tool. Run it from an empty scratch folder; your numbers will differ on your own store, and that is the point of rerunning it there.

Write the threshold and the four questions. Each carries its id, the asker's group, the question and the top three clauses with their scores, exactly as the bench prints them at rung 4. In your own build these come straight from the retriever of session 5.

The abstain rule. Two hand-off conditions, checked in order, each with its own reason string. The reason goes on the ticket, so it must say which condition fired.

★ handoff.py · part 2, the ruledef decide(top): if not top: return "hand_off", "nothing the asker may read matched" if top[0][1] < THRESHOLD: return "hand_off", f"top score {top[0][1]:.2f} is under {THRESHOLD}" return "answer", f"top score {top[0][1]:.2f} clears {THRESHOLD}"

The one tool. It takes a payload, opens at most one ticket per question ref and returns the ticket id. Here "opening" prints the JSON; in production it is one call to your ticketing system's create endpoint, with the ref as its duplicate key.

★ handoff.py · part 3, open_ticketopened = {} def open_ticket(payload): # The one tool. One question, at most one ticket. if payload["ref"] in opened: return opened[payload["ref"]] ticket_id = f"TKT-{len(opened) + 1:04d}" opened[payload["ref"]] = ticket_id print(json.dumps({"ticket": ticket_id, **payload}, indent=2)) return ticket_id

Wire them together. Answer or hand off for each question, route by the top clause's domain, then retry G07 on purpose to prove the tool does not open a second ticket.

★ handoff.py · part 4, the loopfor a in asked: action, why = decide(a["top"]) if action == "answer": print(f"{a['id']}: answer, cite {a['top'][0][0]} ({why})") continue route = a["top"][0][0].split("-")[0] if a["top"] else "HELPDESK" ticket = open_ticket({ "ref": a["id"], "question": a["q"], "asker_group": a["asker"], "top_clauses": [f"{c} {s:.2f}" for c, s in a["top"]], "why": why, "route_to": route, }) print(f"{a['id']}: hand off, {ticket} ({why})") print("retry G07 ->", open_ticket({"ref": "G07"})) print(len(opened), "tickets opened for", len(asked), "questions")

Run it. python3 handoff.py prints exactly this. Two answers, two tickets, and the retry returns TKT-0001 instead of opening a third.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Builder session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Rung 3 answers 41 of 42 answerable questions correctly. Why is it still NO-GO?

Rung 3 has no abstain step, so the dog, birthday and staff party questions get whatever scored highest. Hit rate is 100.0% on every rung, leaks are 0 from rung 2, and thumbs-up is 90.7%. Adding the threshold makes it rung 4: right hand-offs 91.7%, GO.

2 · Why are the ticket's top clauses taken from the list after the permission filter?

The hand-off message tells the asker what was looked at. Built from the filtered list, it can never show more than an answer could. The threshold is not a permission control: on rung 1 the salary band clause scores 7.02, far above any line.

3 · Why does code, not the model, decide to call open_ticket?

OWASP's LLM01 names indirect prompt injection from files, and RAG does not fully mitigate it. Keeping the decision in code removes the tool from the model's reach. D overreaches: actions like unlocking accounts need more tools, and that is a different product with its own go/no-go.
