Ship It: Internal AI Assistant · Leader session 5 of 6

What it costs to run

The first question a finance director asks about an internal assistant is what the model will cost. For Tidewell Freight, a constructed 1,200-person freight forwarder, the honest answer is a range: SGD 0.25 to 8.53 a month on an assumed 2,400 questions. That is the small line. The big line is the hand-off tickets the assistant opens when it should not answer, SGD 6,000 a month on the same assumptions. Tonight you price both, label every input, and learn why the cheapest version of the assistant is the one the go/no-go board rejects.

Part 1 · covers model cost per answer, built from tokens and an assumed price range

Tokens as a range

6 min live

A hosted model API charges by the token, separately for what you send in and what comes back. Every answer Tidewell's assistant gives sends the same three things in: a block of system instructions, the staff member's question, and the three clauses retrieval chose. Count those, multiply by a price, and you have the cost of one answer. Each step in that sentence hides an assumption, so the result is a range, never a number.

Put together: one answer costs USD 0.00011 to USD 0.0036. The range is wide on purpose. Prices change faster than a business case is read, and the right model tier for a policy assistant is a decision you have not made yet. A single number would look precise and be wrong by the time it reached the board.

Self-studyWhy the system prompt is most of the bill3 min▶

Of the 465 average input tokens, 350 are the system instructions, by assumption: the rules about citing the clause, answering only from what was retrieved and handing off when nothing fits. They are sent with every single answer. The question and the three short clauses Tidewell's store holds are the smaller part, because Tidewell wrote its policies one short rule to a clause.

So the levers that move model cost are dull ones: a tighter system prompt, fewer clauses per answer, shorter clauses. None of them should be pulled before the go/no-go board has been re-run, because each one also changes what the assistant can answer. Part 3 shows why a saving that moves the verdict is not a saving.

Real world

The cost slide that quoted one price (constructed). In Tidewell's constructed history, the first business case for the assistant priced it at the cheapest per-token rate the project team had seen, and called the model cost "negligible". Both parts were defensible, and neither survived the first question from the finance director: which model tier, and at whose price next year? The second version of the slide carried the range, USD 0.00011 to 0.0036 per answer, with the word "assumption" next to every input. It was approved faster, because nobody had to trust a number they could not check.

Part 2 · covers the monthly model cost against the cost of hand-off tickets

The ticket line

6 min live

Multiply one answer by a month and the model cost is still small: SGD 0.25 to 8.53 at rung 4, on an assumed 2,400 questions. Session 4 made the assistant hand off when it should not answer, and every hand-off is a helpdesk ticket that a person closes. Price the ticket and the picture turns over.

LiveWhere the SGD 6,000 comes from3 min▶

Three inputs, each labelled. Measured: at rung 4 the assistant hands off 15 of the 54 golden questions, 11 rightly and 4 as safe misses. Assumed: that share holds for 2,400 real questions a month. Assumed: a ticket takes 12 minutes of helpdesk time at SGD 45 an hour, SGD 9 a ticket. Together that is SGD 6,000 a month in hand-off tickets, against SGD 0.25 to 8.53 in model cost.

The four safe misses (G07 pay day, G17 laptop refresh, G37 carry forward, G38 remote days) are questions the assistant could have answered and handed off instead. Each is a ticket the queue absorbs. Session 4's threshold note is therefore also a budget line: raising the hand-off line makes the model bill smaller and the ticket bill bigger.

LiveSGD 21,600 is a ceiling, not a saving3 min▶

The third bar prices every one of the 2,400 questions as a ticket. It is tempting to subtract the hand-off line from it and call the gap a saving. Do not, for two reasons. First, the gap assumes every answered question would otherwise have become a ticket. Tidewell's constructed log held 600 tickets in a whole quarter; 2,400 questions a month is an assumption about how often staff ask once asking is easy, and a question asked because it is easy was never a ticket. Second, the gap counts every answer as right. Part 3 deals with that.

Put your own helpdesk's real monthly ticket volume next to the hand-off line before anyone writes the word saving. If the assistant opens more tickets than the helpdesk closes today, the business case is about speed and consistency for staff, not about cost, and it should say so.

Self-studyWhat the published evidence will not tell you about the saving3 min▶

The two most-cited field studies measure different things and point in different directions. Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond (NBER Working Paper 31161) studied 5,179 customer support agents with an AI assistant and found issues resolved per hour rose 14% on average, 34% for novice and low-skilled workers, with minimal impact on the most experienced. Humlum and Vestergaard (BFI Working Paper 2025-56) followed 25,000 Danish workers in exposed occupations and found average time savings of 3%, with effects on earnings and recorded hours larger than 1% ruled out.

One is a task-level speed gain in one contact centre; the other is the absence of a measurable labour-market effect. Different outcomes, settings and horizons. Neither is a deflection rate, and no credible public benchmark for helpdesk deflection was found; the figures in circulation are vendor-reported. Keep productivity percentages off the run-cost sheet. Session 6 takes the disagreement apart.

Real world

Two studies, two answers, one tempting slide. A business case that wants a big saving can quote the 34% gain for novice agents from Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond. One that wants caution can quote the 3% average time savings from Humlum and Vestergaard. Both are real and read at source; neither was measured on an internal policy helpdesk. The professional move is to show the ticket line you can price from your own log and leave the productivity percentage out of the cost sheet.

Part 3 · covers why cost belongs next to the go/no-go verdict, not apart from it

Cost per right answer

5 min live

Lay the bench's rungs side by side with their run cost and the cheapest month is obvious: rung 1, no filters and no hand-offs, so no ticket line at all. It is also the assistant that leaks restricted clauses to 18 questions. Cost per question rewards it. Cost per right answer does not.

LiveWhy the cheap rungs are cheap3 min▶

Rungs 1 and 2 never hand off. Every question gets an answer, so there is no ticket line and the model cost is a little higher, SGD 0.34 to 11.78 a month, because every question is sent to the model. Rung 4 sends fewer questions to the model, SGD 0.25 to 8.53, and opens SGD 6,000 of tickets. On a cost-per-question slide rung 1 wins by a wide margin.

Now read the board. Rung 1 answers 18 questions from clauses the asker may not read, 7 of them straight from the forbidden clause, and cites 6 superseded policies. Rung 2 stops the leaks and cites 7 stale versions. Both are NO-GO under Tidewell's gate. The cost of a leaked HR clause or a wrong expense limit is not on the run-cost sheet at all, which is exactly why it cannot be the only sheet on the table.

LiveThe sentence for the business case2 min▶

Compare configurations only among the ones that pass the go/no-go board, then compare them by cost per right outcome: a correct cited answer, or a right hand-off. On Tidewell's bench only rung 4 passes, with 38 of 42 answerable questions correct and 11 of 12 right hand-offs. Its run cost is the model range plus the ticket line. A cheaper rung that fails the board has no cost per right answer worth quoting.

Thumbs are no shortcut either. Rung 1 scores a 90.7% thumbs-up rate, above Tidewell's 75% bar, with 18 leaks. Staff thumb down hand-offs, so the safe rung grades lower, 81.5%. A satisfaction score per pound spent would pick the wrong assistant twice over.

Self-studyEstimating before launch vs measuring after2 min▶

This page takes the pre-launch side: an estimate from the bench with every input labelled, good enough to sign a budget. Measuring real token use per request, cost per conversation and drift once staff are using it belongs to the AI observability course's session on what AI costs you. Plan to replace every "assumption" on your sheet with a measured value in the first month after launch, starting with questions per month and the hand-off share.

Real world

The risk that has no line on the invoice. OWASP's Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 lists Sensitive Information Disclosure as LLM02 and asks teams to limit access to sensitive data "based on the principle of least privilege". Rung 1 breaks that principle on 18 questions and is still the cheapest month on the bench. A cost sheet cannot price a disclosure, so it must never be read without the board beside it.

Build-along

A run-cost sheet with every input labelled

★ 20 min · everyone builds

A spreadsheet or a sheet of paper is enough. Use your own helpdesk's volumes and ticket cost if you have them; otherwise build Tidewell's sheet from the numbers on this page. Tidewell is constructed, and your numbers will differ. The rule for the whole sheet: no input without a label saying measured, assumed or constructed.

Set the volume and label it. Questions per month is the input everything else multiplies. If you have no pilot, it is an assumption; write it down as one. Note next to it how many tickets your helpdesk closes in a month today, so nobody confuses the two.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · On Tidewell's bench at rung 4, which line dominates one month's run cost?

A and B are true statements about the model bill, and the whole model bill is still the small line. D is a ceiling for comparison, not a cost the assistant incurs. The hand-off tickets a person has to close are the big line.

2 · Rung 1 is the cheapest month on the bench. Why is it not the configuration to cost the business case on?

B is true by a few dollars and beside the point: with no ticket line, rung 1 is far cheaper overall. C is backwards: rung 1 scores 90.7% thumbs-up, which is the anti-lever. D is backwards too: rung 1 hands off nothing. It is cheap because it never says "I don't know".

3 · A colleague subtracts the hand-off line from the SGD 21,600 ceiling and calls the gap the monthly saving. What is wrong with that?

2,400 questions a month is an assumption about how often staff ask once asking is easy; Tidewell's own log held 600 tickets in a quarter. And a confident wrong answer saves nothing. B fixes a rounding error and keeps both mistakes. C imports a number measured on a different job.
