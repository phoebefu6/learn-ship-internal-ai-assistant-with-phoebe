Part 1 · covers the go/no-go decision and who sets its thresholds

The go/no-go

6 min live

A go/no-go is a list of checks with a line on each, written down before anyone sees the results. Tidewell's board runs the assistant four times, adding one safeguard per rung, and every rung but the last fails for a reason you can read off the row.

LiveThresholds are a policy choice, so somebody signs them3 min▶

Tidewell's lines are its own choice, not an industry standard, and the page should say so. They were written down before any rung ran:

CheckTidewell's lineWhy that shape
Leaks0One answer from a clause the asker may not read is a disclosure, not a quality issue (session 3)
Stale citations0Quoting a retired policy is a wrong answer with a real clause attached
Confident wrong answersAt most 5% high risk, 10% medium, 20% lowTolerance falls as the cost of being wrong rises; the golden set carries 21 high, 19 medium and 14 low-risk questions
Correct answersAt least 70% of the 42 answerableThe assistant must be worth asking
Right hand-offsAt least 80% of the 12 it should not answerSaying "I don't know" is a feature that has to work (session 4)

Rung 3's printed reasons read like a memo: medium-risk 31.6% over 10.0%; low-risk 35.7% over 20.0%; hands off 8.3%, needs 80.0%. Rung 4 clears every line with 38 of 42 correct (90.5%) and 11 of 12 right hand-offs (91.7%). Its one confident wrong answer is G54, the staff party question, low risk, top score 3.71. A board member who wants zero wrong answers is asking for a different threshold, and the honest reply is to move the line and show what it costs in hand-offs.

LiveWhere the board sits in NIST's four functions3 min▶

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework organises the work into four functions: Govern, which is cross-cutting, then Map, Measure and Manage. The go/no-go touches all four, which is why it belongs to a named leader and not to the build team alone.

FunctionOn Tidewell's launch page
GovernWho chose the thresholds, who signs, who owns each document, who receives an incident report
MapThe scope sentence and the three ticket bins from session 1; the access groups from session 3
MeasureThe board: leaks, stale citations, correct, hand-offs and wrong answers by risk tier
ManageThe hand-off, the version register, the nightly diff and the incident threshold

This page takes one side of a seam: the go/no-go for this one assistant. The general gating method and risk tiers in depth lives in the agent launch course's risk tiers session.

Self-studyThe grade that passes the assistant you rejected3 min▶

A thumbs-up button is the cheapest signal you will ever collect, and the bench shows why it cannot be the gate. Graded by thumbs-up, with GO at 75%, rung 1 scores 90.7% and passes: the assistant with 18 leaks and 6 stale citations. Thumbs then fall as safety rises, from 90.7% on rungs 1 to 3 to 81.5% on rung 4, because staff thumb down hand-offs. The thumbs signal on the bench is constructed, but the direction is the lesson: satisfaction rewards the confident answer the board exists to stop. Keep thumbs as a reason to read a conversation, never as the verdict.

Real world

NIST puts Govern first and across everything. The AI RMF 1.0 (NIST AI 100-1) describes Govern as the cross-cutting function, the one that informs Map, Measure and Manage rather than following them. Read for an internal assistant, that is a plain instruction: write who owns the thresholds and the documents before anyone runs a test, or the test has nobody to report to.

Part 2 · covers document owners, the version register and the incident threshold

Owners and versions

6 min live

The GO on the board is true on the day it was measured. Policies change after that. The assistant keeps answering from whatever its index says is current, so the question after launch is not "is the model good" but "who tells the index that a policy changed, and who checks it listened".

LiveThe register is the truth; the index is a copy3 min▶

Tidewell's constructed store holds 49 clause versions across 16 documents: 42 current and 7 superseded versions still sitting in the store. Every clause carries a stable key, a version, an effective date, a superseded flag, an owner and an access group. The version register is the record each owner keeps of which version is in force. The index is what retrieval reads, and it is built from the register, so the two agree until one of them changes alone.

The break button on the bench shows the failure in one move: index the superseded expenses policy (FIN-EXP v4) as current. The register says version 4 was retired on 2026-02-01. The pipeline sees 0 stale citations and prints GO. The register view finds 1: G41, the per diem question, answered with SGD 50 from version 4 instead of the SGD 60 daily allowance in version 5. At rung 3 the same break produces 2 register-stale citations, G38 and G41, and the pipeline still sees 0. Version checks read the register; a nightly job diffs the index against it.

LiveWhat a document owner signs up for2 min▶

Session 1 put any topic without a named owner on a waiting list. Tonight the owner gets a job description, short enough to say yes to:

  • Marks a version superseded in the register on the day the new one takes effect, not when the intranet page is updated.
  • Reads the nightly diff report when it names one of their documents.
  • Sets the access group per clause, never by folder (session 3).
  • Reviews the hand-off tickets on their topics once a month: each one is a question the policy did not answer.

Tracing and monitoring the running system in depth belong to the AI observability course; the after-launch method for agents in general to the agent launch course's after-launch session. This page takes the narrow side: owners and versions for one policy assistant.

Self-studySet the incident threshold before the first incident3 min▶

Singapore's Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI (IMDA and AI Verify Foundation, 2024) lists Incident Reporting as one of nine dimensions, and is blunt about the hard part: "Defining 'severe AI incidents' or setting the materiality threshold for formal reporting is therefore key." For an internal assistant the threshold can be written in the vocabulary the board already uses. Tidewell's constructed version, as a template:

EventTidewell's class (constructed)Goes to
An answer drawn from a clause the asker may not readSevere: report formally[data protection lead], [AI owner]
A citation to a superseded version on a high-risk questionSevere: report formally[document owner], [AI owner]
A stale citation on a low-risk question, caught by the nightly diffLogged, fixed, counted monthly[document owner]
A confident wrong answer with no leak and a current citationLogged; threshold reviewed if the rate moves[AI owner]

What counts as reportable under law is a separate question for the PDPA course and the GDPR course; Tidewell's policies are not legal advice.

Real world

The property that makes updating easy also makes it silent. Lewis and colleagues' 2020 retrieval-augmented generation paper showed that a model's knowledge "can be easily updated at test time" by swapping the document index, without retraining. That is why a policy assistant can follow a policy change overnight. It is also why it can follow a mistake overnight: one wrong flag in the index changes the answer with no model change to review. The register and the diff are the review.

Part 3 · covers the published field evidence on AI help for support work

Contested evidence

5 min live

Somebody at the launch meeting will ask what the assistant is worth. The two most careful studies anyone is likely to quote point in different directions, and they are both right about what they measured. Put the disagreement on the page instead of picking a side.

LiveWhat each study measured, and what it cannot tell you3 min▶

Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond (NBER Working Paper 31161) followed 5,179 customer support agents given an AI assistant. Issues resolved per hour rose 14% on average and 34% for novice and low-skilled workers, with minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers. The published version in the Quarterly Journal of Economics (140(2), 2025) reports 5,172 agents and 15% on average, with small declines in quality for the most experienced. Cite the version you read; the 34% belongs to the working paper.

Humlum and Vestergaard (BFI Working Paper 2025-56) looked at 25,000 workers in 11 exposed occupations in Denmark and found "precise zeros" on earnings and recorded hours, ruling out effects larger than 1%, with average time savings of 3%. The NBER version (w33777) rules out effects larger than 2%.

One measures task speed inside one contact centre; the other measures pay and hours across a labour market, over a different horizon. The literature has not reconciled them and a launch page should not pretend to. Neither study measured an internal policy assistant answering HR, IT and finance questions.

LiveThe sentence for the launch page2 min▶

Promise what you will measure, not what someone else measured. A defensible line reads: "Published field evidence disagrees on the size of the benefit; we will report, after one quarter, the share of policy-lookup questions answered and cited, the hand-off tickets opened, and whether the same person reopened the same topic." Session 1's ticket split and session 5's cost sheet give the baseline for every number in that sentence.

Self-studyWhy no deflection number appears anywhere on this page2 min▶

Helpdesk assistants are often sold on a deflection rate: the share of questions that never became a ticket. No credible public benchmark for it was found; the figures in circulation are vendor-reported. It is also easy to game, because a person who gives up counts as deflected, and an assistant that never hands off scores best. On Tidewell's bench that assistant is rung 1, with 18 leaks. Rung 4, the one that passes, opens 15 hand-offs on 54 golden questions by design.

Real world

Two careful studies, one board meeting. A finance director quoting "+34%" and an HR director quoting "3% time savings, no effect on hours" are both citing real, read-at-source results. The first comes from customer support agents and resolutions per hour; the second from earnings and recorded hours across Danish occupations. The useful move in the meeting is to name what each measured, then point at the quarter-one report your own launch page commits to.

Build-along

The one-page launch decision

★ 20 min · everyone builds

Bring the five sheets from sessions 1 to 5. If you skipped some, fill those blocks with Tidewell's numbers from this page and the earlier sessions, mark them constructed, and write the decision for Tidewell. Your numbers will differ; the shape of the page should not.

Copy blocks 1 to 5 in, one line each. The scope sentence, the layer you build, the leak count after the filter, the hand-off threshold, and the monthly run cost as a range. If a block is empty, write "missing" in it: a launch page with a visible gap is better than one that hides it.

Write the thresholds with the name of whoever chose them. Use Tidewell's lines as a starting point and change any you would argue for differently.

★ Block 7a · the gate, Tidewell styleThresholds chosen by: [name, role], on [date], before any run. Leaks 0 Stale citations 0 Confident wrong answers at most 5% high / 10% medium / 20% low risk Correct answers at least 70% of answerable questions Right hand-offs at least 80% of questions it should not answer Thumbs-up rate reported, never a gate

Paste the board result rung by rung, with the reason each NO-GO failed. A reader should see why the most correct rung did not ship.

★ Block 7b · the board (Tidewell, measured on the bench)Rung 1 no filter NO-GO 18 leaks, 6 stale, 0.0% hand-offs Rung 2 + permissions NO-GO 7 stale, 0.0% hand-offs Rung 3 + version filter NO-GO medium 31.6% > 10.0%; low 35.7% > 20.0%; hand-offs 8.3% < 80.0% Rung 4 + hand-off GO 0 leaks, 0 stale, 90.5% correct, 91.7% right hand-offs Known wrong answer at GO: G54 staff party, low risk, top score 3.71

Write block 6: owners, register, diff, incidents. One row per source document. A document with no owner does not go live; it goes back to the waiting list from session 1.

Homework

Before you sign

30 min
  1. Finish the one-page launch decision and read it to one person who did not attend: if they cannot say why the most correct rung was rejected, rewrite block 7b.
  2. Ask the owner of your most-asked policy when the current version took effect and where that date is recorded. If the answer is "the intranet page", you do not have a register yet.
  3. Write your incident threshold in four rows: what is severe, what is logged, who receives each, and how fast.
  4. Find the last productivity or deflection claim made for an assistant in your organisation and write, next to it, what was measured, in what setting and over what horizon.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Rung 3 answers 97.6% of answerable questions correctly, more than rung 4's 90.5%. Why is rung 3 NO-GO on Tidewell's board?

Rung 3 already has 0 leaks and 0 stale citations, and its thumbs-up rate is 90.7%. It fails on hand-offs (8.3% against 80.0%) and on medium-risk (31.6% against 10.0%) and low-risk (35.7% against 20.0%) wrong answers. Correctness on answerable questions says nothing about the questions it should not have answered.

2 · After launch, the superseded expenses policy version 4 is accidentally indexed as current. The pipeline's own check still prints GO with 0 stale citations. What should the version check read?

The index agrees with itself, which is why the pipeline sees 0 stale citations. Only the owner's register says version 4 was retired, and it finds G41 answering SGD 50 instead of SGD 60. Re-indexing from a wrong source repeats the mistake on schedule.

3 · A board member asks how much productivity the assistant will add. Which answer belongs on the launch page?

A mixes the working paper's 34% with the journal citation, which reports 15% on 5,172 agents. B has no credible public benchmark behind it. C generalises one contact centre's resolution speed to a different job; Humlum and Vestergaard found 3% time savings and no measurable earnings or hours effect.
