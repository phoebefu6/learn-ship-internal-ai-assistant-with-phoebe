Ship It: Internal AI Assistant · Leader session 2 of 6

Buy, build or wait

The pitch for an internal assistant usually arrives as a product: a model, a search box, a chat window, ready in weeks. All three are real and most of it is worth buying. What the pitch leaves out is the part that decides whether the assistant is right and safe: your policy clauses, the people who own them, who may read each one, and the ticket it opens when it cannot answer. Tidewell Freight, a constructed 1,200-person freight forwarder, is the running case. You leave with one verdict per layer, and a test for when the honest answer is "not yet".

Part 1 · covers the layers that stay yours whatever you buy

What you cannot buy

6 min live

Split the assistant in two. On one side, the machinery: a model that writes a sentence, a retriever that finds the best clause, a window where staff type. On the other, three layers that only your organisation can supply. The machinery is for sale. The layers are not, and the machinery is only as good as what it reads.

LiveThe three layers, and what each one is made of4 min▶
LayerWhat it isTidewell (constructed)Why no product supplies it
The corpusYour policies cut into clauses, each with a stable key, version, effective date, superseded flag and owner49 clause versions across 16 documents: 6 HR, 5 IT, 5 finance; 42 current, 7 superseded still in the storeOnly your organisation knows which version is in force and who keeps it true
Access groupsAn access group on every clause, so the assistant can filter before it ranksAll staff, managers (managers and HR), HR only, finance only, IT onlyA product can enforce groups; it cannot decide them
The hand-offThe ticket the assistant opens when it should not answer, routed to the right teamOne action only: open a ticket with the question, the asker's group and the clauses it looked atThe queue, the teams and the routing rules are yours

Notice that none of the three is software. They are data and decisions. That is why "buy" sounds like a short project and is not: the purchase is quick, and the work it depends on is the work you would have had to do anyway.

LiveWhat "retrieval-augmented" means for the buyer2 min▶

Most internal assistants are built on retrieval-augmented generation. Lewis et al. (2020) defined it as models "which combine pre-trained parametric and non-parametric memory for language generation": the model is the parametric part, and the index of documents it retrieves from is the non-parametric part. For a buyer that sentence is the whole point. The model is a commodity you rent. The non-parametric memory is your corpus, and the vendor ships it empty.

Singapore's IMDA framework for generative AI names retrieval-augmented generation among techniques "commonly used to reduce hallucinations and improve accuracy". Commonly used is not guaranteed: it reduces wrong answers only as far as the documents it retrieves are current, permitted and on topic. Those three conditions are the three layers above.

Real world

The memory you can swap without retraining. Lewis et al. showed that a retrieval-augmented model's knowledge "can be easily updated at test time" by replacing its document index, with no retraining. It is a useful property and a clear warning. The same swap that lets you correct the assistant overnight lets a stale or mislabelled document change its answers overnight too. Whoever owns the index owns the answers, and that is you, not the seller.

Part 2 · covers the buy, build or wait verdict for each layer

Build what is yours

6 min live

Do not decide buy or build for "the assistant". Decide it five times, once per layer, and name each answer as a tool class rather than a vendor. Prices change monthly; the split between what you rent and what you own does not.

LiveWhy the model is the easy row3 min▶

On the bench, the shipped assistant reads about 465 input tokens per answer. Priced across the bench's assumed range, that is USD 0.00011 to 0.0036 per answer, or SGD 0.25 to 8.53 in model cost for a month of 2,400 questions. The hand-off tickets the same assistant opens cost SGD 6,000 a month at Tidewell's assumed SGD 9 a ticket. Every input here is an assumption, labelled as one, and session 5 takes the sheet apart. The shape survives any reasonable price: the model is the small line.

So spend the selection effort where the risk is. A model choice that is wrong costs a few dollars to reverse. A corpus that cites a superseded policy, or an access layer that lets a staff member read a managers-only clause, costs trust that a cheaper model cannot buy back.

LiveRetrieval and chat: buy, or build small2 min▶

This is the one row with a real choice. An enterprise search product brings connectors and a chat window; a document store with access control plus a thin page of your own brings less, but every step stays inspectable. Either is reasonable. The test for both is the same, and it comes from OWASP's 2025 list for LLM applications: under LLM08, "Implement fine-grained access controls and permission-aware vector and embedding stores." Ask the seller, or your own team, to show the filter running before ranking, on your access groups, with a question a staff member must not get answered. Session 3 builds that test.

Which retrieval method to use, chunking, embeddings and reranking, is not a leader decision and is not taught here: the RAG course owns it.

Self-studyHow this connects to the playbook's verdict and data gate3 min▶

The four-way verdict decides whether a use case is worth pursuing at all and in what form, and the data gate checks whether the data can carry it. Run those first if nobody has. This session assumes the answer was "yes, an assistant", and applies the same discipline one level down: a verdict per layer, and a data check that is specific to policy documents, which Part 3 turns into the wait test.

Real world

A permission layer is not a feature you tick. On the bench, Tidewell's first rung has no permission filter. Retrieval is perfect by its own measure, every answerable question finds its clause in the top three, and 18 questions still leak a clause the asker may not read; 7 of them are answered from that clause. The bought machinery worked exactly as designed. The access layer was missing, and only the organisation could have supplied it. Tidewell is constructed; the leak count is measured on its bench.

Part 3 · covers the conditions under which the right answer is to wait

When to wait

5 min live

Wait is a verdict, not a failure. If the corpus cannot carry an assistant, buying one only makes the gaps answer back in a confident voice. Three checks decide it, and failing any one of them means wait and fix the corpus first.

LiveThe three checks, and why each one is a stop3 min▶
CheckWait ifWhy it stops the purchase
OwnersDocuments with no named person who keeps them currentAn assistant quotes an orphaned policy with the same confidence as a maintained one, long after it stopped being true
Access metadataClauses with no access group, or groups inherited from a folder nobody reviewedNo product can filter on a label that does not exist; it will either show everything or hide everything
Written answersMore than half the topics staff ask about have no written policyThe assistant spends most of its time on questions it should hand off, and a ranking always finds a best match to answer from anyway

The half line is a judgement for your organisation, not a published standard. Tidewell's constructed quarter sits far below it: 44 of 600 tickets had no written answer anywhere. Use session 1's tally to place your own.

Self-studyWaiting is work, not a pause2 min▶

A wait verdict comes with a to-do list: name an owner for every document, have each owner set an access group per clause, write the missing policies or a sentence saying there is none, and retire superseded versions from the store. Every item is useful even if the assistant never ships, because the same gaps already cost the helpdesk minutes today. Set a date to rerun the three checks, and treat the list as the project plan.

Who should set access, and why it should be per clause rather than inherited from a folder, is session 3. The access model itself belongs to the data access control course.

Real world

A constructed case: the old expenses policy, indexed as current. On Tidewell's bench there is a button that indexes the superseded expenses policy (FIN-EXP v4) as if it were in force. The shipped assistant's own checks still say GO and see 0 stale citations. Read against the version register, the verdict is NO-GO: one answer quotes a per diem of SGD 50 from v4 instead of the SGD 60 daily allowance in v5. No product would have caught it, because the only record of which version is current was the organisation's own register. Tidewell is constructed; the result is measured on its bench in Builder session 8.

Build-along

Your buy, build or wait sheet

★ 20 min · everyone builds

One page, five rows, one verdict per row and one overall call. Bring your scope sentence from session 1. No data to hand? Fill it in for Tidewell from the numbers on this page; your numbers will differ, and the rows will not.

Copy the five rows. Model, retrieval and chat, corpus, access groups, ticket hand-off. Do not add a vendor column. If a seller is already in the room, their product goes in the tool-class cell as a class: "an enterprise search product".

★ The empty sheetLAYER VERDICT TOOL CLASS OWNER WAIT IF model ________ ________________________ ____________ ____________ retrieval and chat ________ ________________________ ____________ ____________ corpus ________ ________________________ ____________ ____________ access groups ________ ________________________ ____________ ____________ ticket hand-off ________ ________________________ ____________ ____________

Fill the corpus row from your scope sentence. List the documents the sentence names. For each, write the owner and the date it last changed. Count how many have no owner. That count, not the vendor shortlist, decides whether the overall call can be anything but wait.

Fill the access row. For each document, write which groups may read it. If the honest answer is "it inherits from the shared drive", write that down; it is a wait condition, and session 3 explains why.

Fill the hand-off row. Name the ticket queue each kind of hand-off goes to and the person who owns that queue. An assistant whose hand-offs land nowhere has turned a safe answer into a lost question.

Run the three checks and write the overall call. Owners, access metadata, written answers. Pass all three and the call is buy or build by row. Fail one and the call is wait, with the fix list and a date to rerun.

★ The overall call, Tidewell (constructed)Overall: proceed. Buy the model and the retrieval layer as tool classes; build the corpus, access groups and ticket connector ourselves. Owners: yes, on every clause. Access metadata: yes, 5 groups. Written answers: 44 of 600 tickets had none, well under half. Before launch: retire the 7 superseded versions still in the store. Rerun the checks before session 6's go/no-go.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 2 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · A seller offers an assistant that "connects to all your documents out of the box". Which part of Tidewell's assistant does that purchase actually supply?

Reading every document is not the same as knowing which version is current, who owns it, or who may see each clause. A and B are the pitch's assumptions. Lewis et al. describe the document index as the model's non-parametric memory; the product ships it empty.

2 · On the bench, model cost for a month of 2,400 questions is SGD 0.25 to 8.53 (assumption) and hand-off tickets are SGD 6,000. What should that do to the buying conversation?

The model is the small line and easy to swap. C is the trap: hand-offs are what stop the assistant answering questions it should not, and session 5 shows the cheapest rung is the one you must not ship.

3 · A department's policies have no named owners and inherit access from shared folders, but fewer than half its asked topics are unwritten. What is the verdict?

Failing any one check means wait. Building instead of buying does not create owners or access groups; both options read the same missing labels. Fixing after launch means staff are already getting answers from unowned, unfiltered clauses.
