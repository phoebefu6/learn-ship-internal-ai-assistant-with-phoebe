Ship It: Internal AI Assistant

One internal assistant, shipped end to end, with every decision in the order you meet it. Tidewell Freight, a constructed 1,200-person freight forwarder, wants a policy and ops helpdesk that answers staff from HR, IT and finance policy, cites the clause it used, and opens a ticket when it cannot answer. Six leader sessions take it from the ticket log to a launch decision: what staff actually ask, buy or build, who may see what, what it says when it does not know, what it costs, and how to keep it true after launch. Eight builder sessions build it: the clause store, BM25 retrieval, golden questions from the tickets, grounded citations, permissions inside retrieval, one tool that opens the ticket, superseded versions, and a go/no-go bench computed in your browser.

Leader track 🧭 · six sessions, no code

For whoever owns, funds or signs off an internal assistant. Scope it from the tickets, decide what it may see and what it says when it does not know, price it, and sign the go/no-go.

🎟️ Leader session 1 · start here

What staff actually ask

Read the tickets before the model. Policy lookups, actions and questions nobody wrote down, three bins that each need a different behaviour, and the scope sentence that follows.

▶ Start here ⚖️ Leader session 2

Buy, build or wait

What a bought assistant brings and what it cannot: your documents, their owners and who may read what. When waiting is the right answer.

 🔐 Leader session 3

Who may see what

Permission-aware retrieval as a leadership decision: filter before the model sees anything, never after. Eighteen leaking questions become none.

 🙋 Leader session 4

What it says when it doesn't know

The hand-off as a product. Why answering less turns a NO-GO into a GO, and the threshold you have to choose with a price on both sides.

 🧮 Leader session 5

What it costs to run

Model tokens as a range, labelled as assumptions, next to the cost of every hand-off ticket. The small line and the big line are not the ones people expect.

 🚦 Leader session 6

Launch, then keep it true

The go/no-go stitched together, document owners and versions after launch, and what the evidence on AI help for support work does and does not say.

Builder track 🔧 · eight sessions, Python and the browser

Tidewell's assistant built part by part in the order the data flows, from the clause store to a go/no-go bench that runs every part at once.

🗂️ Builder session 1 · start here

The corpus is the product

The clause as the unit of retrieval and citation, eight fields per clause, old versions kept and flagged, and a validator that refuses a bad store.

▶ Start here 🔎 Builder session 2

Chunk, index, retrieve

One clause per chunk, BM25 computed by hand, top-3 retrieval, and why the best match is not always a good one.

 🥇 Builder session 3

Golden questions from the ticket log

Fifty-four questions drawn from real ticket topics, each with the asker's group, the gold clause, a risk tier, and the ones that should get no answer at all.

 📎 Builder session 4

Grounded answers with citations

Answer only from what was retrieved, cite the clause, and check that the citation is in the retrieved set and is the current version.

 🛡️ Builder session 5

Permissions inside retrieval

The permission filter as code, applied before ranking, and the leak count that proves it works.

 🎫 Builder session 6

One tool: open the ticket

The abstain threshold and the single tool call: a ticket that carries the question, the asker's group, the clauses looked at and why it handed off.

 🗓️ Builder session 7

Versions and superseded policies

The owner's version register against the index's flag, the current-version filter, and the nightly diff that catches a re-imported old policy.

 🎛️ Builder session 8

The go/no-go bench

Four rungs from 18 leaks to GO, a thumbs-up grade that passes the unsafe assistant, and a break button that produces a GO the register contradicts.
